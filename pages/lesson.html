📖 Understanding Agree/Disagree Essays

What is an Agree/Disagree Essay?

An Agree/Disagree essay is a type of IELTS Writing Task 2 where you need to state your opinion on a given topic and provide reasons and examples to support your view.

Key Characteristics:

  • You must clearly state your opinion
  • Provide 2-3 main supporting arguments
  • Include relevant examples and evidence
  • Use formal academic writing style
  • Aim for 250+ words

Two Main Approaches

1️⃣ One-Sided Approach

You completely agree or completely disagree with the statement.

Structure:

  • Introduction: Introduce the topic and clearly state your opinion
  • Body Paragraph 1: First reason + example
  • Body Paragraph 2: Second reason + example
  • Body Paragraph 3: Third reason + example (optional)
  • Conclusion: Restate your opinion and summarize

2️⃣ Balanced Approach

You acknowledge both sides but lean more towards one opinion.

Structure:

  • Introduction: Introduce topic, mention both views, state your opinion
  • Body Paragraph 1: Arguments for the opposing view
  • Body Paragraph 2: Your arguments (stronger)
  • Body Paragraph 3: Your additional arguments
  • Conclusion: Restate your opinion

📝 Essay Templates

Template 1: One-Sided Approach

Introduction:

"The statement that [topic] is [agree/disagree]. I completely [agree/disagree] with this view for several important reasons."

Body Paragraph 1:

"Firstly, [first reason]. For example, [specific example]. This clearly demonstrates that [connection to topic]."

Body Paragraph 2:

"Secondly, [second reason]. In fact, [evidence/example]. Therefore, [conclusion of paragraph]."

Conclusion:

"In conclusion, I believe that [restate opinion]. As discussed, [briefly summarize main points]."

Template 2: Balanced Approach

Introduction:

"While some people believe that [opposing view], I hold the view that [your opinion] is more convincing."

Body Paragraph 1 (Counter-argument):

"It is true that [opposing argument]. Some argue that [evidence]. However, this argument is less persuasive than [your position]."

Body Paragraph 2 (Your argument):

"I strongly believe that [your opinion]. Firstly, [reason 1]. For instance, [example]."

Conclusion:

"To sum up, although [acknowledge opposing view], I am convinced that [your opinion] for the reasons discussed above."

💡 Useful Linking Words & Phrases

To introduce ideas:

  • Firstly, Secondly, Finally
  • To begin with, Moreover, Furthermore
  • In addition, Additionally

To show contrast:

  • However, Nevertheless, On the other hand
  • While, Although, Despite this
  • On the contrary, Conversely

To give examples:

  • For example, For instance
  • Such as, Like, In particular
  • To illustrate, A case in point

To conclude:

  • In conclusion, To conclude
  • To sum up, In summary
  • Therefore, Consequently

✅ Checklist for Success

  • ☐ Clear thesis statement in introduction
  • ☐ 2-3 well-developed body paragraphs
  • ☐ Each paragraph has one main idea with supporting examples
  • ☐ Use formal vocabulary and appropriate tone
  • ☐ Vary sentence structures
  • ☐ Use linking words appropriately
  • ☐ Conclusion restates main idea
  • ☐ 250+ words
  • ☐ Proofread for grammar and spelling errors

🎯 Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • ❌ Not stating a clear opinion
  • ❌ Switching opinions between paragraphs
  • ❌ Weak or irrelevant examples
  • ❌ Repetitive ideas across paragraphs
  • ❌ Too casual or informal language
  • ❌ Grammatical errors
  • ❌ Very short essay (less than 250 words)
  • ❌ Unclear connection between ideas
